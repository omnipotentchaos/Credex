# User Interviews — CredexAudit

Three interviews with real people.

---

## Interview 1

**Participant:** Krishna  
**Role:** Student  

### Key quotes
- “What exactly is this company doing? The idea of trading AI credits is interesting.”
- “How are the credits actually being transferred behind the scenes? Like, how does the marketplace work?”

### Most surprising thing he said:
Krishna is technical, but what stood out was that he cared more about the business model than the actual audit itself. He barely focused on the savings report. Instead, he kept digging into how Credex works operationally and how the marketplace could realistically function.

### What it changed about my design
That conversation made me realize my original CTA was too abrupt. Users needed more context before being asked to take action. So I rewrote the CTA banner to briefly explain that Credex enables “verified credit transfers, often 10–30% below retail pricing.” That small change made the flow feel much clearer and more trustworthy.

## Interview 2

**Participant:** Piyush  
**Role:** Student  
**Company stage / team size:** Solo  

### Key quotes
- “I’m genuinely surprised by how detailed this assignment is. The economics and GTM docs are intense.”
- “I’ve always wanted to work on a project structured like this, where you actually have to think about the business side too.”

### Most surprising thing he said
What surprised me most was that Piyush spent more time reading the documentation and architectural decisions than exploring the UI itself. Features like PDF export and shareable links impressed him, but the thing that really pulled him in was the depth of the supporting docs, especially ECONOMICS.md

### What it changed about my design
His feedback shifted how I thought about the project presentation. I realized that for an assignment like this, strong documentation is just as important as the product experience itself. After that interview, I reworked the README.md to surface the engineering decisions, trade-offs, and supporting docs much more prominently.

## Interview 3

**Participant:** Akanksha  
**Role:** Software Engineer  
**Company stage / team size:** Expedia Group (3.5 yrs experience)  

### Key quotes
- “At a company like Expedia, we actually have a huge problem tracking AI spend across different teams.”
- “If I used this at work, I couldn’t buy the credits myself. I’d need a way to send these results directly to my manager or finance team.”

### Most surprising thing they said
The seat right-sizing and overpaying checks caught her eye. Apparently, leaving unused seats on enterprise plans is an everyday headache for her. But then she flagged something big: the developer running the audit isn’t the one paying for the credits.

### What it changed about my design
That was a wake-up call. Because of her, I added the shareable URL feature and transactional email support. Now, instead of just showing local results, the app can generate a unique link or email the findings straight to a manager — making the whole approval chain smoother and helping engineers get those savings approved faster.
